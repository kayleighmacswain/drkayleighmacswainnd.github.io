What Your Lab Results Aren't Telling You

By Dr. Kayleigh MacSwain, ND

You've been told your bloodwork is "normal." But you still feel exhausted, foggy, or off. Sound familiar?

Standard lab reference ranges are designed to flag disease — not to optimize health. They're based on population averages, which means you can fall within "normal" and still have plenty of room for improvement.

The difference between normal and optimal

Take ferritin (stored iron) as an example. The standard reference range might list anything above 12 µg/L as normal. But research suggests that many people don't feel their best until ferritin is above 50–70 µg/L. If yours is 15, you'll be told you're fine — but you might be running on empty.

The same applies to thyroid markers, vitamin D, B12, and many others. Functional medicine uses tighter, evidence-based optimal ranges to catch these gaps before they become clinical deficiencies.

What standard panels often miss

  • Full thyroid panel: Most standard panels only test TSH. A complete picture includes free T3, free T4, and thyroid antibodies.
  • Nutrient levels: Vitamin D, B12, magnesium, zinc, and iron are rarely tested together — but deficiencies in any of them can cause significant symptoms.
  • Inflammatory markers: hs-CRP and homocysteine can reveal low-grade inflammation years before it shows up as disease.
  • Fasting insulin: Blood sugar can look normal while insulin resistance is already developing. Fasting insulin is a much earlier warning sign.

How I approach lab work

When I review labs — whether they're new or ones you've had for years — I look at the full pattern, not just individual markers. I also consider your symptoms, history, and goals alongside the numbers. A result that looks fine in isolation might tell a different story in context.

I can also order additional testing through naturopathic requisitions, including specialty panels not typically available through standard care.
